Team kit

Train your own network, then watch it move.

Your team trains a small neural network on a laptop. The result is one file of learned weights. You load that file into the Lab and see your network plan real shots. No account, API key or ChatGPT Work subscription is needed.

Unzip the whole kit and keep the assets/ and student/ folders side by side. Host solutions and the final test scenes are not in the kit.

Ground rules

  • The change must come from learned weights. The network's prediction of the next state has to come from what it learned. Copying the simulator's equations, swapping in a physics model, or asking the simulator to pick the shot does not count.
  • Known geometry is allowed as an input or to route between branches, as long as you say you used it.
  • Change one thing at a time and use a new run name every time. Keep every run, including the ones that fail.
  • Write your hypothesis first, and what result would prove it wrong, before you run anything.
  • Do not change the planner, the scenes, the seed or the scoring. They stay fixed so results can be compared.
  • Report everything: new successes and new failures, not just the wins.
  • Do not look for the final test scenes or the hosts' solution. The hosts score your frozen run once on separate scenes.

Five steps

  1. Install once, before the session

    Open a terminal in student/. PyTorch is a large download, so do this on good Wi-Fi ahead of time.

    python -m pip install -r training-requirements.txt

  2. Train a baseline

    This trains the plain starter network, scores it on the 24 development scenes, and saves a run folder.

    python run_experiment.py --name baseline

  3. Change one thing

    Write your hypothesis first, including what result would prove it wrong. Then change a single factor and compare against the baseline. Use a new name every time.

    python run_experiment.py --name contacts --contact-weight 5 --compare baseline

    The output lists new successes and new failures. A change can make things worse; keep that result and explain it.

  4. Load your weights in the Lab

    In the Lab, use Try your team’s trained model and choose student/results/runs/<your-run>/network.json. The model selector switches to Your team neural network. Find a shot and execute it.

  5. Hand in one run

    Give the hosts your run folder, any code you edited, and a short note: your hypothesis, what disproves it, what you saw, and what got worse.

Questions teams ask

What is network.json?
The learned weights and biases of your network, plus the scaling numbers it needs, saved as text. It is about 60 KB. It is the trained model, not a picture or a report.
Is my file uploaded anywhere?
No. The Lab reads it in your browser tab using the file picker. Nothing is sent to a server, and it disappears when you close the tab. Training itself runs only on your laptop.
How long does training take?
Seconds. The starter trains for 40 short passes over 24,000 examples on the CPU, which took about 2–6 seconds in rehearsal. A whole run, including scoring all 24 scenes, took about 15 seconds on a laptop-class CPU. The slow part is the one-off PyTorch install, not the training.
What score should my baseline get?
About 2 out of 24 development scenes in rehearsal. The original network shown in the Lab scores 0/24, so do not mix the two up. A change that scores 0/24 or worse than your baseline is a normal result: keep it and explain what it rules out.
Do I need a GPU or a paid account?
No. CPU is enough, and no API key or ChatGPT Work subscription is needed.
Can I edit the code?
Yes. The files to edit are train_repair.py (layers, loss, data, training), neural_model.py (inputs and inference) and repair.py (the submission interface). Do not change the planner, the scenes or the scoring.
What counts as a valid repair?
The change in state must come from learned weights. Copying the simulator’s equations, substituting a physics model, or querying the simulator to rank actions does not count.
